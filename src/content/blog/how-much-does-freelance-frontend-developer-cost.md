A founder in Denver emailed me last week: "What's your rate for a new marketing site?" Fair question. Useless question, too — until we know scope. Freelancer pricing pages that list a single hourly number hide the real answer: frontend cost depends on what you're building, how much design is finished, who updates content after launch, and whether performance and SEO are non-negotiable or "nice to have."

I've priced and delivered freelance frontend projects for US, UK, and EU clients for years. This article is the budget conversation I wish every prospect had before they open five browser tabs of developer profiles. If you're comparing quotes — or deciding whether custom frontend is even the right investment — use this as a calibration guide, not a price list.

## What You're Actually Paying For

When you hire a freelance frontend developer, you're not buying hours of typing. You're buying:

- **Translation** from design or brand ideas into a maintainable UI
- **Architecture** that stays fast as content and features grow
- **Integration** with your CMS, CRM, analytics, or payment tools
- **Risk reduction** — fewer launch bugs, fewer SEO regressions, fewer rebuilds in eighteen months

Cheap quotes often skip the last three. That's why a $2,500 site can quietly become a $15,000 rebuild when paid ads scale or the marketing team needs a CMS.

For the decision of *whether* you need custom work at all, see [When to Hire a Frontend Developer vs. Use a Page Builder](/blog/when-to-hire-frontend-developer-vs-page-builder). This post assumes you've already decided custom (or hybrid) frontend is on the table.

## 2026 Price Ranges by Project Type

These ranges reflect fixed-project and milestone pricing I see and charge in the US/UK/EU market for mid-career freelance frontend specialists — not staff-aug body shops and not celebrity agency rates.

| Project type | Typical range | Timeline |
|--------------|---------------|----------|
| Marketing site (5–10 pages), design provided | $4,000–$12,000 | 2–5 weeks |
| Marketing site + headless CMS | $8,000–$18,000 | 4–8 weeks |
| Landing page system for paid ads | $3,000–$8,000 | 1–3 weeks |
| SaaS marketing site + blog | $10,000–$25,000 | 6–10 weeks |
| React/Next.js product UI (MVP screens) | $15,000–$45,000 | 6–14 weeks |
| E-commerce storefront customization | $8,000–$30,000 | 4–12 weeks |
| Performance remediation only | $2,500–$8,000 | 1–3 weeks |
| Monthly retainer (after launch) | $1,500–$5,000/month | Ongoing |

Junior freelancers and offshore agencies undercut the low end. Senior specialists with niche proof (Core Web Vitals, Figma accuracy, ecommerce checkout) sit at the high end. Agency markup on the same work is often 1.5–2.5x.

## What Moves a Quote Up or Down

**Design readiness.** A locked Figma file with tokens, responsive frames, and component states lowers cost. "We'll figure it out as we go" raises it — design exploration during development is expensive.

**Content readiness.** Placeholder copy is fine for structure. Missing product pages, legal pages, and SEO metadata late in the project stretch timelines and burn fixed-budget goodwill.

**Stack complexity.** A content site on Astro is usually cheaper than an authenticated Next.js app with role-based access, charts, and Stripe billing. Stack choice should follow the brief — I walk through that in [Choosing a Frontend Stack for Your Business](/blog/choosing-frontend-stack-for-your-business).

**Integrations.** HubSpot forms, Calendly, Shopify, Sanity, Auth0, analytics tag managers — each one is a real workstream. "Just embed it" still needs testing, consent handling, and performance budgeting.

**Performance and accessibility targets.** "Make it nice" is different from "mobile Lighthouse 90+, WCAG 2.2 AA on key flows." Specifying targets early prevents disputes later.

**Rush timelines.** Compressing a six-week project into two weeks usually means overtime premiums or cut scope — not free compression.

## Hourly vs Fixed vs Retainer

**Hourly** works for undefined audits, advisory calls, and "help us debug for a day." It fails when deliverables are clear and you need budget certainty.

**Fixed / milestone** works when scope is written down: pages, breakpoints, CMS schemas, acceptance criteria. Good freelancers pad unknowns and list exclusions. Bad freelancers underquote and invent change orders.

**Retainer** works after launch when marketing ships weekly campaigns, landing pages, and content experiments. Paying per emergency forever costs more than a predictable monthly cadence.

If you're unsure which model fits, ask the freelancer to recommend one based on clarity of scope — not based on which model maximizes billable hours.

## Red Flags in Cheap Quotes

- No discovery or written scope before price
- "Unlimited revisions" with no definition of revision
- No mention of mobile performance, SEO basics, or handoff docs
- You don't own the repo or hosting credentials
- Everything is a black-box page builder with no custom code ownership when you asked for custom frontend

Cheap isn't automatically bad. Vague is bad. A $5,000 quote with crisp deliverables can outperform a $15,000 quote written on a napkin.

## How I Scope Before Quoting

I don't send a number from an Instagram bio rate. Discovery covers:

1. Business goal for the site in the next twelve months
2. Who edits content after launch
3. Must-have integrations
4. Design status (Figma, brand kit, reference sites)
5. Success metrics (speed, conversions, SEO, launch date)

Then I send a proposal with deliverables, timeline, assumptions, and out-of-scope items. About a third of prospects learn they need a smaller first phase — or a page builder — and I tell them that. Honesty saves both sides a bad project.

## Budgeting as a Business Owner

Treat frontend spend like tooling that multiplies traffic you've already paid for. If you spend $8,000/month on ads and your landing page loads in five seconds on mobile, frontend modernization often pays for itself faster than increasing ad budget.

A practical budgeting approach:

- **Phase 1:** Core pages + performance baseline + analytics
- **Phase 2:** CMS, blog, and secondary templates
- **Phase 3:** Experiments, personalization, and advanced integrations

Phasing keeps cash flow sane and surfaces learning before you overbuild.

- **Scope:** remediation only on category + PDP templates
- **Range:** performance-remediation band ($2.5k–$8k typical)

When prospects ask me to match a $1,800 offshore quote for the London-shaped project, I explain the missing lines: redirects, CWV acceptance, CMS modeling, and ownership of the repo. Cheap becomes expensive when those lines appear as emergencies.

## Comparing Quotes Without Getting Played

Build a scorecard before you fall for the lowest number:

| Criterion | Weight | What good looks like |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| Written deliverables | High | Page list, breakpoints, integrations |
| Performance targets | High | Mobile LCP/INP/CLS on named URLs |
| Ownership | High | You own code, hosting, design files |
| SEO/migration notes | Medium | Redirects + metadata if replacing a site |
| Communication cadence | Medium | Staging reviews, single feedback channel |
| Price | Medium | Fits phase budget; not the only axis |

A mid-priced proposal that names [Core Web Vitals](/blog/optimizing-core-web-vitals-modern-frontend) acceptance criteria often beats a bargain that ships a pretty, slow site you'll redo after ads scale — the trap described in [How Businesses Lose Customers to Slow Websites](/blog/how-businesses-lose-customers-slow-websites).

## Geographic and Engagement Nuances

I work with clients across US, UK, and EU time zones. Pricing correlates more with seniority and scope clarity than passport stamps — but expectations differ:

- **US startups** often want milestone pricing and aggressive launch dates
- **UK agencies** white-labeling frontend (see related agency workflows) need quieter communication and stricter fidelity
- **EU product companies** frequently ask for accessibility and privacy/consent rigor in the same breath as visual polish

None of that changes the core math: undefined scope costs more everywhere.

## What Should Be In Writing Before Money Moves

Minimum proposal contents I recommend you demand — from me or anyone else:

1. Deliverables checklist (templates, CMS schemas, integrations)
2. Assumptions (design frozen by date X; content by date Y)
3. Explicit exclusions (brand redesign, copywriting, photography)
4. Acceptance criteria (browsers, breakpoints, CWV targets)
5. Revision policy (rounds counted; what counts as a change request)
6. IP and repo ownership at each milestone payment
7. Support window after launch (days of bugfix vs new features)

If a freelancer won't write those down, you're not buying a project — you're buying hope.

## Retainer Economics After Launch

A founder in Austin once told me they preferred one-off emergency invoices because retainers "felt expensive." Six months of Friday-night landing-page panics cost more than a modest monthly cadence with a backlog.

Retainer sweet spots:

- **Light ($1.5k–$2.5k):** occasional landers, small fixes, advisory
- **Standard ($2.5k–$4k):** weekly campaign support + CMS help
- **Heavy ($4k–$5k+):** design-system evolution, multiple brands, ongoing experimentation

If you don't have a backlog two weeks in a row, pause or shrink — a good freelancer won't invent busywork to protect the invoice.

## DIY, Freelancer, or Agency?

| Option | Best when | Hidden cost |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| DIY / page builder | Simple site, low traffic stakes | Ceiling on speed/brand; your time |
| Freelance specialist | Clear frontend outcome, design ready | You still own product decisions |
| Agency | Multi-discipline (brand+dev+CRO) | Markup; coordination overhead |

Freelance frontend is the wrong buy when you still need brand strategy and positioning workshops. It's the right buy when design (or a clear brief) exists and you need production-quality UI that stays fast. ## Conclusion

Freelance frontend pricing in 2026 is not a secret menu. Marketing sites with clean design and clear scope land in the mid four to low five figures. Application UIs and commerce customization climb from there. The variance between quotes is usually scope quality, seniority, and whether performance, SEO, and maintainability are included.

Ask for deliverables, ownership, and acceptance criteria — not just a day rate. The right developer will help you spend less than the wrong stack forced you to rebuild.
